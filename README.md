# KRYPTOS: Geospatial & Structural Analysis

A technical breakdown of the coordinate puzzle in Kryptos, focusing on the 1990 installation context, geodetic alignment, and spatial relationships between the sculpture, USGS control points, and the K3 coordinates.

---

## 📍 Kryptos K3 Coordinates (1990 Location)

The coordinates encoded in Kryptos Part 3 are:

38°57′06.5″ N  
77°08′44″ W

When plotted using the NAD83/WGS84 datum (the standard used by USGS and CIA contractors in 1990), the point falls:

- South of the Kryptos sculpture  
- Inside the CIA New Headquarters Building courtyard  
- Approximately 100 feet south of the sculpture’s copper screen  

This point is not the sculpture itself — it is a distinct location within the courtyard.

---

## 📐 USGS Survey Marker (Control Point)

The USGS control point referenced in Kryptos research is the one used to align the CIA orthophoto imagery.

Key facts:

- It was a survey control point inside CIA grounds  
- It is not a public NGS benchmark  
- Its exact PID is not publicly released  
- It was used to georeference the orthophoto that allowed accurate plotting of the K3 coordinates

## 🧭 Orientation of the Kryptos Copper Screen (1990 Installation)

At installation (Nov 3, 1990), the sculpture was oriented as follows:

- The encrypted copper screen faces east/southeast  
- The curved “scroll” form runs north–south  
- Kryptos sits in the northwest corner of the courtyard  

This orientation has remained unchanged since installation.

## 🧲 Magnetic Declination in 1990

Magnetic declination at Langley, VA was different in 1990 than today.

Why this matters:

- Kryptos includes a lodestone compass rose  
- Magnetic north drifts over time  
- East‑coast declination typically changes 0.1°–0.2° per year  

Thus, the compass alignment Sanborn used in 1990 was several degrees different from current values.

## 📏 Geometric Line Between Marker, Sculpture, and Coordinates

Plotting the three points:

- USGS survey marker  
- Kryptos sculpture  
- K3 coordinates  

Creates a diagonal NW → S alignment across the courtyard.

This forms a triangular geospatial relationship symbolizing:

- Reference point (survey marker)  
- Origin of the clue (sculpture)  
- Destination of the clue (coordinates)  

This geometric alignment reinforces Kryptos’ themes of mapping, intelligence gathering, and hidden spatial relationships.
